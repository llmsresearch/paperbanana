TITLE: The Spike, the Sparse and the Sink: Anatomy of Massive Activations and Attention Sinks  
AUTHORS: Shangwen Sun 1, Alfredo Canziani 1, Yann LeCun 1, Jiachen Zhu 1 (+ New York University)  
VENUE: unknown  
ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY: The co-occurrence of massive activations and attention sinks in Transformer models is an architectural artifact caused by pre-norm configuration, and the two phenomena serve distinct functions—massive activations act globally as implicit parameters, while attention sinks modulate attention locally across heads.  
PROBLEM:  
- Massive activations and attention sinks frequently co-occur in modern Transformers, but their functional roles and causal relationship are unclear.  
- These phenomena impact practical tasks like quantization, pruning, and long-context inference, yet remain poorly understood.  
METHOD:  
- Analyze post-residual hidden representations to identify massive activations.  
- Use RMSNorm with pre-norm configuration to study activation propagation.  
- Examine attention mechanisms in multi-head self-attention blocks.  
- Compare model behaviors under different normalization configurations (pre-norm vs. post-norm).  
- Identify step-up and step-down blocks that generate and neutralize massive activations.  
RESULTS:  
- Massive activations appear only in intermediate layers and a small number of channels.  
- They exhibit fixed inter-channel ratios and are confined to a few tokens.  
- Step-up blocks introduce extreme values early; step-down blocks cancel them near the end.  
- Attention sinks attract disproportionate attention mass regardless of semantic relevance.  
- Abolishing pre-norm decouples massive activations and attention sinks.
