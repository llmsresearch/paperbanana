TITLE: Human-AI Shared Control via Policy Dissection  
AUTHORS: Quanyi Li⋄‡, Zhenghao Peng§, Haibin Wu⋄, Lan Feng†, Bolei Zhou§ (⋄Centre for Perceptual and Interactive Intelligence, †ETH Zurich, ‡University of Edinburgh, §University of California, Los Angeles)  
VENUE: NeurIPS 2022  
ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY: Policy Dissection enables human-AI shared control by aligning intermediate neural representations with kinematic attributes without retraining or modifying the policy, achieving 95% success rate and almost zero cost in unseen traffic scenes.  
PROBLEM:  
- Autonomous agents lack generalizability in unseen environments, limiting real-world deployment.  
- Neural controllers are opaque and not controllable, reducing safety and trustworthiness in safety-critical systems.  
METHOD:  
- Conducts frequency analysis to match time series of neural unit activations with kinematic attribute variations.  
- Identifies motor primitives as units whose activation correlates best with specific kinematic attributes.  
- Builds a stimulation-evoked map linking motor primitive activation to desired behaviors.  
- Uses only rollouts of a pretrained policy to extract internal representations.  
- Does not modify the reward function, observation design, or retrain the policy.  
RESULTS:  
- In autonomous driving, the human-AI shared control system achieves 95% success rate and almost zero cost in unseen near-accidental traffic scenes.  
- Locomotion robots exhibit versatile controllable motion skills (e.g., tip toe, crouch, back flip) despite being trained only to move forward.  
- Novel behaviors can be deliberately evoked by activating units related to certain kinematic attributes.  
- The method enables zero-shot task transfer in test-time unseen environments.  
KEY NUMBERS:  
- 95%: Success rate of human-AI shared control in unseen traffic scenes.  
- Almost zero cost: Safety cost in unseen traffic scenes under shared control.  
- 95% success rate and almost zero cost: Performance metrics in autonomous driving task.  
- Zero-shot task transfer: Capability demonstrated in locomotion tasks.
